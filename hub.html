Starter Kit

New here? Start with this.

New to the programme? Read this first — it covers everything you need to know. Refer back to the FAQ at the bottom for anything else.

As a Global Student Ambassador, you help promote elite student competitions — including the Harvard Crimson Global Essay Competition, Harvard Crimson Case Competition, the LSE Generate Global Innovation Challenge, and the High School Academic Research Competition (SARC) — to your school, your friends, and your wider community.

You set aside 1–2 hours a week to share and promote our competitions with your peers. Every sign-up made with your personal code builds credit toward credit vouchers and mentoring.

You earn $3 USD in credit for every registration you bring in. Once you've built up $60 (20 registrations), you can cash it out as a credit voucher — or exchange it for an hour of 1:1 mentoring on university applications, essays, extracurriculars, and interview prep.

Sign-ups carry over from one competition to the next. There are also weekly bonus credits and round-end prizes for the top ambassadors.

You can add "Global Student Ambassador – powered by Crimson Education" as a position on LinkedIn under Volunteer Experience or Experience. Include your start date and describe it as "promoting elite international student competitions to peers globally, developing leadership and outreach skills."

Ambassador Promotional Content Brief
Flyers, cold emails, social media posts, and more — open the content brief.

The Harvard Crimson Global Case Competition is a prestigious virtual business competition inviting high school students worldwide to step into the shoes of a CEO or consultant and develop strategies to solve real-world business challenges facing top global companies.

Hosted online in partnership with Crimson Education and The Harvard Crimson, the competition builds industry-ready analytical, strategic, and presentation skills — connecting students with a global network of like-minded peers. Last year, 4,200+ students competed on the global stage.

Who is it for?

  • Designed for students aged 13–18 worldwide — as it's an online competition, there are no geographical barriers.
  • Students register individually and then form teams of 2–4 people.
  • Open to students currently enrolled in secondary school, including gap-year students. Anyone already enrolled in university/college is not eligible.

How does HCGCC work?

  • Register individually during the open enrollment period, then nominate a team leader to complete the team registration form.
  • Learn new skills for free through on-demand bootcamps and live webinars with industry experts, beginning 17th October.
  • Qualifiers: submit a written case-solution deck (5–8 Google Slides, plus a max. 5-slide appendix) judged on issue diagnostics, analysis, scope of innovation, and validation. 50 teams (10 per region across 5 regions) advance.
  • Regionals: submit a video presentation (max. 5 minutes, all members visible and speaking) judged on presentation logic, engaging speaking, technical organization, and teamwork. The top 2 teams per region (10 teams total) advance to the Global Finals.
  • Global Finals: finalists receive a new case and present it LIVE to a panel of judges in a virtual event, with a deeper 8–15 slide deck.
  • Get recognized and win incredible prizes!

As a Global Ambassador, you'll be promoting four international student competitions:

Harvard Crimson Global Essay Competition (HCGEC) — an academic essay competition that challenges high school students worldwide to research and write persuasively on topics across a range of disciplines.

Harvard Crimson Global Case Competition (HCGCC) — a business case competition where students work in teams to analyse a real-world business problem and pitch a solution, building analytical and presentation skills.

LSE Generate Global Innovation Challenge (LGIC) — formerly Pitch for the Future, run in partnership with LSE Generate, this challenge invites students to design solutions to real-world problems through the lens of the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

High School Academic Research Competition (SARC) — a research-focused competition giving students the chance to conduct and present original academic research with guidance from mentors.
